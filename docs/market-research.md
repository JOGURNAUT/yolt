# Market research: resale platforms and pricing for India

Desk research from September 2026 on how comparable platforms work, what went wrong for the ones that left or changed course in India, and what that means for YOLT's positioning and fees. Decisions that came out of it are recorded in [decisions.md](decisions.md) (D13, D16).

**Limits of this research:** it's based on public articles, investor memos, help pages and fee guides. There were almost no forum discussions or app reviews to read (Oyela's original app listing has been removed). Buyer and seller behaviour still needs to be checked in interviews ([seller-interview-guide.md](seller-interview-guide.md)).

## 1. Oyela (India, 2021–present)

A Gurugram startup founded by IIT Bombay alumni, often remembered as "a really good thrift app". It wasn't acquired: it pivoted twice.

| Phase | What it was | How it worked |
|---|---|---|
| 2021–22 | Peer-to-peer resale app ("shop from friends") | Listing in about 2 minutes with dropdowns and price suggestions; likes and reposts; **drops** (virtual selling events) and **groups**; automatic shipping labels; size guidance from brand size charts; ₹500–700 items aimed at tier 2–4 cities; **instant payouts** to sellers using a risk score instead of waiting out the return window; credit lines for sourcing stock. Reported 4,000+ sellers, 100k monthly active users, 20% monthly repeat buyers. |
| 2023 | Tools for Instagram stores | Storefronts, automated order collection from Instagram DMs and comments, collaborations between stores, a "Frontway" seller app. ₹14.4 Cr seed round (Prime Venture Partners, October 2023); 20k+ Instagram stores; projected $3M GMV for FY24. |
| Now | Its own new-fashion store | oyela.in sells new clothes from its own stock at ₹199–1,899 with Buy 1 Get 1 offers and "no question asked" returns. FY25 revenue ₹2.3 Cr. |

**Likely reason for the pivots (inference, not confirmed):** a ₹500–700 order with a small commission barely covers shipping, payment fees, returns and support. Selling its own stock earns a margin; brokering other people's items only earns a small cut.

**What to keep:** fast listing, drops, community features, fast payouts. **What to avoid:** drifting away from the one thing people loved.

Sources: [Good Capital](https://www.goodcapital.vc/gc-blog/why-we-invested-in-oyela), [Eximius](https://eximiusvc.com/blogs/eximius-vc-backs-oyela-investment-thesis/), [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2023/10/prime-venture-partners-leads-seed-round-in-oyela/), [Indian Startup News](https://indianstartupnews.com/stories/this-gurugram-based-startup-helps-new-age-social-media-stores-growth-their-business-2317219), [Inc42](https://inc42.com/company/oyela/overview/), [oyela.in](https://oyela.in/).

## 2. Poshmark in India (2021–2023)

Poshmark launched in India in 2021. Naver acquired it in 2023 at about $1.2B, down from a $7.3B valuation. In October 2023 it closed India, the UK and Australia with **one week's notice**; the three markets together had fewer than 500k monthly active users.

**Lessons:** a US playbook didn't transfer to Indian shipping, pricing and trust. Sellers remember being dropped at a week's notice, so YOLT should promise a listings export and a long notice period for any shutdown or major policy change.

Source: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/19/poshmark-international/).

## 4. Positioning for a price-sensitive market

**Compete on trust and fit, not price.** Meesho and Shein-style apps own "cheapest", and a one-of-one item that needs photos, measuring and single-item shipping can't be the cheapest option. Oyela's move to new stock with Buy 1 Get 1 offers shows where a price race leads.

What stops price-sensitive buyers from buying off Instagram isn't the price tag. It's the fear of losing ₹800 to a scam page, or getting a size that doesn't fit. YOLT removes both risks: payment through the platform, and real measurements with photographed flaws.

**Positioning line:** "Thrift without the DMs: every piece measured, every flaw shown, your money protected."

**Starting audience:** metro college students and young professionals, where Instagram thrift culture and prepaid payment habits are strongest. (Oyela's tier 2–4 buyers were COD-heavy. This is an inference to test, not a verified finding.)

## 5. Fees: why not a buyer fee (yet)

Vinted's buyer-fee model works because its sellers are mostly individuals with no other shop, so buyers can't find the same item cheaper elsewhere. YOLT's early sellers are Instagram stores: the same jacket is ₹800 in their DMs. A buyer fee would make YOLT more expensive than DMs, and it would push the stores' own followers back to Instagram. That's the traffic YOLT most needs early on. Mercari's experience (fees moved to buyers, transactions fell) points the same way.

**Model adopted (D13, D16):**

| Phase | Buyers | Sellers | Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | ₹0 | ₹0 | None. Budget about 2% of GMV for gateway fees, plus refunds and support. |
| Growth | ₹0; same price as Instagram | ₹0 on sales from their own share links and followers. A small commission (5–8%, to be tested) **only on buyers YOLT found for them** through search, feed, drops or recommendations. | Discovery commission, and Seller Pro (Instagram import, bulk tools, analytics, faster payouts) |
| Later, maybe | Small protection fee | — | Only if individual closet sellers with no other shop become most of the supply |

**Attribution rule:** the first visit decides. A visit through a seller's share link marks that buyer as the seller's own for 30 days, and those orders carry no commission. Sellers see in their dashboard which sales came from YOLT.

**Other levers:**
- **Free shipping on a buyer's first order,** funded by YOLT: a better hook than any fee is a barrier.
- **Bundling:** "Add another piece from this store and save ₹79". Shipping is the largest cost on a ₹500 item.
- **No size returns** (decided after this research, D6): like Vinted, Depop and Carousell. Reverse shipping (₹80–100) is 15–20% of a ₹500 item. Buyer protection covers not-as-described, damaged, wrong-item and never-arrived instead.
- **Fast payouts** are what sellers valued in Oyela. Start with payouts after the return window closes, then offer faster payouts to sellers with a good track record (a Seller Pro feature).

## 6. Open questions to test

1. Which commission rate on YOLT-found buyers do sellers accept? (Interview question 44.)
2. Do buyers notice or care about any fee at all when protection is explained? Test only after liquidity.
3. What share of early orders comes from sellers' own links versus YOLT discovery? This decides when revenue starts.
4. Does free first-order shipping convert Instagram followers into repeat YOLT buyers, or only into one-off orders?
